# From patient influences to stopping boundaries

Choose one estimator for monitoring and orient its test statistic so that positive values favor treatment. For ratio and odds, use the log effect and its corresponding patient influence. For net benefit and net win time, use their original scales. This calculation uses the joint null distribution across looks for that *one* statistic. Independently monitoring several estimators and stopping on any crossing would require a different joint design.

## Covariance

At each tested look save one influence per enrolled patient, the permanent patient ID and randomized arm. For look j, divide the patient's influence by the number enrolled in that arm at j. Match these scaled influences by ID at j and k, multiply them, and sum over patients and arms. A subject enrolled only at k contributes zero. This gives the covariance of the estimators themselves, with neither a further factor of n nor an n−1 correction. It includes the IPCW or Aalen–Johansen nuisance contributions already constructed by the corresponding engine.

Historical estimates, influences and critical values stay fixed even if more information becomes available. A new look adds one row and one column to the covariance; it does not re-estimate the earlier block. The square root of the current diagonal is the standard error. Standardize each covariance entry using its two diagonal variances to obtain the correlation of the Z statistics. A zero or nonfinite variance prevents testing. Nonmonotone information alone is not a failure; no canonical information-fraction formula is used.

## Spending and sidedness

Spending levels are cumulative across **scheduled** looks; they are not nominal p-value cutoffs. Suppose j was the last performed test and the target cumulative spending at k is alpha[k]. Under the fitted centered multivariate normal, the probability of having stayed inside every previous tested boundary **and** now crossing is alpha[k] − alpha[j]. This is an unconditional probability. Equivalently, the probability of staying inside every tested boundary, including the candidate boundary at k, is 1 − alpha[k]. Use that equation to solve for the current cutoff while leaving earlier boundaries unchanged.

A two-sided continuation coordinate is [−c,c], and an upper-only coordinate is (−∞,c]. The design can be two-sided throughout, upper-only throughout, or upper-only at interim looks and two-sided at the scheduled final look. In the mixed case the final magnitude c is symmetric, but the earlier one-sided stopping region makes the total upper and lower crossing probabilities generally unequal. The code does not assign half of the remaining unconditional error to each tail. The clinical stopping condition is strict crossing (>); equalities have zero probability under the continuous reference law.

A skipped look is absent from the calibration history and spends nothing. If the last performed test spent 0.01, an interim scheduled for cumulative 0.025 is skipped, and the two-sided final target is 0.05, the final first-crossing probability must be 0.04. With independent first and last Z statistics this yields 2.0495942664: 0.99 × 2 × Pr(N(0,1) > 2.0495942664) = 0.04. With correlation 0.5 and an upper-only first boundary 2.3263478740, the same final total requires 2.0322231051. If both interims were skipped, the first performed test at the final look uses 1.9599639845 at overall two-sided 0.05. If the final test is also unsupported, the trial concludes without rejection.

## Internal calculation and contracts

All probabilities below refer to **continuation**. A critical value is on the standardized Z scale. The absolute error budget is on the probability scale, not the Z scale.

- **`.gpc_cov(history, previous = NULL)`.** Takes a list in tested-look order. Each element contains `subjects`, a data frame with character `id`, integer/numeric 0/1 `arm` and finite numeric `phi` on that estimator's test scale. A patient ID occurs once in a look and retains its arm across looks. Returns a K × K symmetric numeric matrix. If given a finite symmetric (K−1) × (K−1) `previous` block, copies it and computes only the current column; the first call may receive an empty matrix. Without it, computes all pairwise cross-products. Invalid input is an error; an empty arm at a candidate look means there is no test. For N subjects per look, building from scratch uses O(NK²) time; appending uses O(NK) time, including validation. Stored subject records and the covariance occupy O(NK + K²).
- **`.gpc_boundary(correlation, previous, directions, alpha, tol)`.** Takes a K × K symmetric correlation with unit diagonal, K−1 earlier critical values in historical order, K directions (`"upper"` or `"two-sided"`), the scheduled cumulative spending and an absolute probability tolerance (default 1e−7). Returns `critical`, `probability`, `integration_error`, `error` and `method`. The total diagnostic `error` adds the absolute deviation from 1−alpha and the estimated integration error. The target tolerance is tightened when necessary relative to the new spending increment. Invalid directions or shapes are input errors. Nonpositive Cholesky pivots, a pivot indistinguishable from zero at the configured machine-precision criterion, insufficient remaining probability, or failure to certify the numerical tolerance defer the test. There is no diagonal ridge, substitution of independent correlations or automatic change of the spending rule.
- **`.gpc_normal_interval(a, b)`.** For aligned scalar or equally long vector limits (allowing infinite endpoints), returns the standard normal probability between each lower and upper limit. Uses upper-tail differences when both endpoints are positive; no process state is changed. O(length(a)).
- **`.gpc_rect2(lo, hi, rho, tol)`.** For two-dimensional numeric lower/upper limits, a nonsingular correlation rho and an absolute tolerance, returns the named probability and quadrature-error estimate `p,e`. The independence rectangle is corrected by integrating its normal-density derivative in the angle from 0 to asin(rho). This is deterministic, supports mixed directions and negative correlations, and avoids subtracting four nearly unit CDFs. Infinite-limit derivative terms vanish; a failed one-dimensional quadrature defers the test. Cost is proportional to the adaptive number of integrand evaluations.
- **`.gpc_legendre(n)`.** Converts a positive integer quadrature order to a list of n nodes and n weights on [−1,1]. Its symmetric tridiagonal Jacobi matrix supplies the nodes and weights; each distinct order is calculated once and cached. Dense eigendecomposition costs O(n³) initially and O(n²) work memory.
- **`.gpc_prefix(L, lo, hi, ngrid, tol)`.** For three or four tested looks, takes the lower Cholesky factor of their K × K correlation and the K−1 fixed continuation limits. Integrates the independent standard-normal innovations generating the earlier Z statistics using a product of Gauss–Legendre rules. Returns the earlier probability masses (`weight`), the corresponding conditional means for the last Z (`mean`), and its common conditional standard deviation (`sd`). These are reused for all candidate current critical values at that grid order. The integration truncates innovations at a normal quantile such that total omitted mass is at most tol/32. An empty preceding rectangle yields zero mass. For fixed K, worst-case size grows as ngrid^(K−1); this is why this route is limited to K ≤ 4.
- **`.gpc_prefix_eval(prefix, lo, hi)`.** Combines the prefix masses with the final conditional interval probabilities at the candidate limits; returns their scalar sum. The prefix weights are not normalized to one: their sum is the probability of reaching the current look. Cost is O(number of prefix nodes), with no refit.
- **`.gpc_rect_qmc(lo, hi, corr, tol)`.** For general K, fixed Gaussian rectangle limits and the positive-definite correlation, calls the Genz–Bretz normal integration in `mvtnorm`, using common randomized integration points for successive boundary candidates. Returns named probability and estimated absolute error `p,e`. Its fixed integration seed and RNG settings are local to the evaluation; the caller's RNG settings and stream are restored. The integration budget is at most two million points per evaluation; the estimated integration error is checked when accepting a root. Empty-width rectangles return zero directly. A failure to compute a finite probability and error defers the test.
- **`.gpc_boundary_root(eval, target, twosided, tol)`.** Accepts a scalar-candidate evaluator returning `p,e`, the scalar target continuation mass, the direction and tolerance. Brackets an increasing probability. The lower endpoint is zero for symmetric boundaries and is extended below zero when needed for upper-only tests. Uses a bracketed univariate solver (Brent interpolation with bisection safeguards) and re-evaluates the accepted root. Returns `critical, probability, integration_error, error`. An unbracketed target defers the test. The expensive step is the probability evaluator; the root solver fits no models.

## Choosing and checking the numerical route

The first tested look uses a normal quantile of the log tail probability and checks the calibration error on the crossing-probability scale. Its returned continuation probability may round to one for tiny spends. Mutually independent subsequent looks accumulate the log probability of reaching the new look and use a log-tail quantile for the additional crossing probability. An increment too small to resolve at the requested tolerance is deferred. Correlated two-look designs use deterministic bivariate integration. At three or four looks the conditional grid is refined from 12 to 24, 48 and, if needed, 80 nodes per coordinate. Both the probability at the fitted cutoff and the earlier continuation mass are compared across neighboring grids. Grid disagreement is a numerical error estimate, not a formal quadrature bound; the known truncation bound is added to it. For three or more looks with a smallest-to-largest correlation eigenvalue ratio below 0.001, nearly redundant constraints can make the estimated error of multivariate integration unreliable. In this case, for four or more coordinates, a weaker standardized inequality can be removed if a Gaussian difference tail bounds the probability it changes the decision. The sum of these bounds is charged to the numerical error. Up to three remaining coordinates are integrated deterministically using univariate, bivariate or trivariate Gaussian probabilities. If a larger remaining system is still nearly dependent, the look is deferred. Other unresolved integrals at three or four dimensions and larger well-conditioned designs use the general multivariate routine. No boundary is accepted when its reported error exceeds the applicable tolerance. A fixed numerical tolerance controls computational accuracy; it is not a proof of finite-sample statistical error control.

The statistical justification assumes a centered joint Gaussian limit under the prespecified null, consistent influence covariance, positive limiting variances, a positive-definite limiting correlation, and negligible probability of deferring a test. It does not guarantee nominal type I error after a frequent outcome-dependent skip. Simulate the complete monitoring schedule under the planned trial conditions to assess the finite-sample probability of crossing a boundary.
